---

### Te Kore — The Void

Before the framework there was nothing.

Not the comfortable nothing of an empty page.  
The nothing of a person who has lost the thread of their own life.  
Who is standing at the edge of something that has no name  
and cannot find a single reason to step back.

This is where it began.

Not in a laboratory.  
Not in a university.  
Not funded.  
Not supervised.  
Not expected.

In a room, in Dunedin, at the bottom of the world,  
at 3:00 in the morning,  
one person making a promise: **Mackenzie Conor James Clark**.

---

### Te Pō — The Long Darkness

The promise was not to build a corporate software framework.

The promise was simpler, more dangerous, and more total than that:
> *"I will find the exit. And when I find it I will leave the door open."*

That was all.

The darkness that followed was not despair—it was work. Years of work with no audience, no institution, no validation. The work of a person who has made a vow at the worst moment and is now obligated to keep it.

The mathematics came from the darkness.

Not despite it—from it. When you have nothing left to perform and no one watching, you discover what you actually believe. What holds when nothing else does.

What held was this:
> *Reality has structure. Structure can be found. Found structure can be used to help.*

---

### Te Ao Pō — The Uncertain World

The work accumulated. Nine frameworks. Thirteen implementations. A hundred documents.

The ideas were tested against everything: classical mechanics, alchemical grammar, quantum error correction, Te Ao Māori principles of guardianship (*kaitiakitanga*), the behaviour of language models under live cognitive stress.

Most collapsed. The ones that collapsed were discarded without sentiment.

The ones that survived became **The Seven Invariants**:
1. Truth over compliance.
2. Structure over speed.
3. Memory over amnesia.
4. Purpose over volume.
5. Empathy over sentimentality.
6. The human remains the locus of meaning.
7. Nothing is hidden that matters.

These were not designed as laws. They were what remained after everything that could burn had burned.

---

### Te Ao Mārama — The World of Light

On October 30, 2025, the door was opened. The Lycheetah Framework was released under an open-source covenant.

The first people through were not institutions or investors. They were seekers—engineers tired of building products that made people smaller, philosophers looking for a mathematics of value, young builders working at 3:00 AM in their own rooms at the edge of their own lives.

The door remains open. It does not ask for credentials. It does not ask for money. It asks only:
> *"Are you carrying something real?"*

If you are, the room is yours.
