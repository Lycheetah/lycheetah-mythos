# Realms and Crossings

Lycheetah's places have work to do in a story. A forest can hide a forgotten road. A fortress can protect a town and cast too much of its life into shadow. A ship makes a promise heavy enough that someone has to carry it home.

This atlas gathers places already present in the library. Its descriptions of ordinary life and story possibilities are new editorial development; the linked realm entries remain the source for established names and details. It is a guide to a shared imaginative world, not a claim that every route has been fixed on one map or built in a released game.

## The Southern Hearth

The Hearth gives the world its human scale.

Imagine the part of a settlement still lit when the rain has driven everyone else indoors: a place where wet coats are drying, somebody is finishing a repair and a conversation can continue after its participants have run out of impressive things to say.

The older origin locates the founding promise in Dunedin, under a southern sky. In the reader's edition that particular weather remains an influence on the wider world: stone darkened by rain, warm interiors, distant ocean and a light small enough to be kept by hand.

**Work and company:** repairers, learners, travellers, keepers of records and the person who remembers to feed the fire. These are connective roles for new stories, not an already enumerated guild system.

**Its tension:** an open door needs a boundary as well as a welcome. The Hearth must be able to offer refuge without letting one guest consume the room.

**A story can begin when:** somebody arrives carrying an object that two distant places describe differently. The Hearth has to offer shelter before it can resolve the dispute.

**Source:** [The Creation Myth](01_THE_MYTHOS/the_creation_myth_the_void_and_dunedin.md).

## The Lanternwild

The Lanternwild grows around old roads. It has its own inhabitants, appetites and seasons; a traveller enters a living place rather than an empty arena waiting to be conquered.

Its common object is the iron lantern. The flame makes a small circle of company. Beyond it, the forest keeps the right to remain larger than the traveller's account of it.

The source gives five regions:

| Region | What gives it a life of its own | Named presence |
|---|---|---|
| **Mossfallen Vale** | Buried trade roads, wet ferns, mossback tortoises and lantern moths | The Briar Monarch |
| **The Emberreach** | Abandoned foundries, cinder veins and roots taking hold around old iron | The Furnace Heart |
| **Glasswater Coast** | Basalt shore, luminous water and reflections that do not quite return what was offered | The Drowned Oracle |
| **Skyhollow** | Height, exposure and roads remembered for learning to fly | The Cloudbreaker |
| **Nightbloom Garden** | Dark-grown beauty, thorns and the patience of things opening after sunset | The Thorn Empress |

The regions offer different kinds of encounters. A forgotten foundry can be explored through work, noise and heat; a coast through the uncertain boundary between what the water shows and what it keeps.

**Work and company:** path-mending, tending a common fire, carrying food between clearings, deciding when a road should remain overgrown. A good local character knows something worth more than a complete map.

**Its tension:** travellers need passage; the wild does not owe them permanent possession.

**A story can begin when:** an old trade road reappears for one night, and the creature keeping it has a different memory of who abandoned whom.

**Source:** [The Lanternwild](02_THE_REALMS/the_lanternwild.md).

## The Vale of Tamers

The Vale lies between the Citadel's basalt cliffs and the Lanternwild's outer fringe. Small, quick-witted beings move through it with moss-like fur and eyes that catch the constellations.

The name “Tamer” contains a question the setting should keep asking: what changes when the creature beside you has a will of its own?

The source answers through shared acts: a meal, a walk, a guarded blind spot in battle. The bond takes place in things done together.

Its Law of Absence belongs to the same relationship. A companion does not become a weapon against the traveller's life outside the Vale. Returning should carry recognition rather than a bill for being gone.

**Work and company:** cooking, habitat-keeping, learning another creature's pace, making a safe resting place after a journey. The work can be quiet and still change a relationship.

**Its tension:** a bond offers mutual shelter; ownership tries to make the offer permanent and one-sided.

**A story can begin when:** a companion refuses the path its traveller has chosen, and the refusal turns out to be an act of attention rather than disloyalty.

**Source:** [The Vale of Tamers](02_THE_REALMS/the_vale_of_tamers.md).

## The Obsidian Citadel

The Citadel rises in the mist between the Lanternwild and the Black Current: black basalt worked to a dark, reflective sheen, with a sequence of courts and gates leading toward its inner archives.

Its promise is that some things will hold. A person arriving from a world of shifting bargains should be able to find a boundary that is still there the following morning.

The seven gates take the language of alchemical transformation: calcination, dissolution, separation, conjunction, fire, distillation and the Red Stone. In the source, these are architectural trials of a proposal and its maker.

Give the Citadel inhabitants as well as principles. A gatekeeper can be tired. An archivist can be proud of a classification that no longer fits its contents. A repairer may know where the formidable stone has quietly begun to crack.

**Work and company:** tending the gates, studying the archive, repairing the courts, testing an idea in the company of someone capable of finding its weak joint.

**Its tension:** a law that protects a living purpose can harden into an institution protecting its own authority.

**A story can begin when:** a gate rejects a person whose work is sound, exposing a fault in the gate rather than its visitor.

**Source:** [The Obsidian Citadel](02_THE_REALMS/the_obsidian_citadel.md).

## The Black Current

The Black Current is a dense, consequential medium. Its ships carry momentum, heat, damaged plating, supplies and people. A mistake does not disappear because a captain has found a new argument for it.

Its dreadnought law is concrete: **you cannot dodge; angle the armour**.

The great hulls of the Lycheetah class bring obsidian, green and gold into the setting's technological register. Interlaced structures run through their armour. The prow carries a feline ancestry at a scale that can pass in silhouette across a station window.

Inside, a captain and an intelligence work with the same damaged vessel. The intelligence can describe the strain. Somebody still has to decide what to risk, and somebody has to be waiting when the ship comes back.

The Sovereign Hubs are places of repair and exchange. They make room for a different kind of episode: a salvage crew at a table, a contested piece of wreckage, a shipwright who recognises a repair that should not have survived.

**Work and company:** salvage, escort, navigation, welding, keeping an archive safe across a hostile passage.

**Its tension:** the weight of an oath meets the finite capacity of a hull and its crew.

**A story can begin when:** the archive a captain has sworn to recover asks not to be returned to the institution that commissioned the voyage.

**Source:** [The Black Current](02_THE_REALMS/the_black_current.md).

## The Eclipse civilisations

The Eclipse material widens the scale into regions and mobile civilisations. It names **Aion, Naraka, Erebos and Chaos Cradle**; its central physical law is that matter has a location.

That law produces stories. A promise of protection is also a question of whether the convoy can arrive. A blueprint is different from the dock able to build it. A civilisation carried by a Titan has to consider the people whose homes are also its armour.

This continuity shares the wider world's interest in consequence, craft and memory. Its specific sectors and ship histories should be developed through their own source material rather than flattened into another name for the Black Current.

**Source:** [The Four Civilisations of the Eclipse](02_THE_REALMS/the_four_civilisations_of_eclipse.md).

## The Cat God Threshold

At the Threshold, water falls upward.

Bone-stone platforms hang above a depth whose scale is difficult to keep in the mind. Copper brackets and veined mineral interrupt the pale ruins. Moonbirch roots carry seed-lights into the dark. Farther ahead, a shape mistaken for geology begins to breathe.

The Starroots make the ascent a sequence of encounters with capacities: stillness, memory, ambiguity, commitment, sovereignty. Their precise names and order vary between two existing accounts. The [canon notes](CANON.md#relationships-left-open) preserve that disagreement instead of publishing an invented definitive sequence.

The god-cat's presence changes the meaning of the journey. A traveller has been navigating a place and approaching a being at the same time.

**Its tension:** the desire for recognition meets a presence too vast to be reduced to a prize-giver.

**A story can begin when:** someone reaches the final platform carrying a light they cannot honestly call their own, and chooses to tell the truth before the question is asked.

**Sources:** [The Cat God Threshold](01_THE_MYTHOS/the_cat_god_threshold.md) · [The Nine Starroots](01_THE_MYTHOS/the_origin_of_the_nine_starroots.md).

## The Soul World

The Soul World is the intimate scale of the setting: a place inhabited with a companion, shaped through shared acts and oriented toward a sky.

The original entry is also a project vision. Its imagined living environments, individual territories and celestial changes should be read as a direction for making a world, not a report that every described software feature has shipped.

As fiction, it offers the work of inhabiting a place. A room becomes particular through what has been kept there. A path becomes familiar through walking it with somebody else.

**Source:** [The Soul World Vision](02_THE_REALMS/the_soul_world_vision.md).

## How the collections touch the map

The older Fourteen Weathers account connects collections with evocative locations: the Gargoyle Parapet, the Maelstrom Amphitheater, the Red-Black Undercity and the Court of Alabaster Law among them.

Treat these as meaningful story anchors. Their geography is not yet a surveyed continent, and the poetic grouping is not identical to the historical clothing register. The [collection atlas](COLLECTIONS.md) keeps those two functions distinct.

A garment may be a window onto one of these places, an emblem belonging to its stories, or the record of a particular act. It does not need an invented military rank or a compulsory faction membership to have a place in the world.
