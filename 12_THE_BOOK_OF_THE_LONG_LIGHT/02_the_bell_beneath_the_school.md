## ✦ I. THE SOUNDING OF THE UNSTRUCK METAL

The bell had no clapper. It hung suspended in an underground cistern eighty feet beneath the central quadrangle of the Mystery School, forged from an alloy of meteoritic iron and cold-rolled lead. For three hundred years, according to the foundation rolls, it had hung in absolute stillness.

When the first note sounded, it did not travel through the air.

It traveled through the bones of the floorboards. Mara felt the resonance enter her heels, rise through her shins, and vibrate the small brass pins in her hair. It smelled of stagnant river mud and scorched parchment.

Across the Great Library, every door slammed in unison.

Not with the rattling bang of a draft, but with the flat, hydraulic finality of a prison bulkhead sealing shut. For five beats of the heart, the grandeur of the School drained away. The grand barrel-vaulted ceiling ceased to reflect celestial constellations. The running fountain in the courtyard stopped mid-arched spray, water hanging like frozen beads of dull wax. Oak looked like rough-hewn utility timber; hand-forged bronze latches turned gray.

## ✦ II. THE TYRANNY OF THE LABEL

Then the architecture began to classify itself.

High above the archways, the ancient inscribed poetry began to scorch away, replaced by sharp, sans-serif glyphs that cut into the limestone:

* `KITCHEN` peeled away, replaced by **FOOD ALLOCATION**.
* `QUIET ROOM` became **CORRECTIVE ISOLATION**.
* `WHERE LIVING THINGS LEAVE EVIDENCE` became **SPECIMEN IDENTIFICATION**.
* `THE SANCTUM OF THE OPEN HAND` became **REGULATED OUTPUT CORRIDOR**.

Tarin dropped his trowel onto the flagstones. The iron blade clanged against stone and left no mark—not even a spark.

"I hate renovations," Tarin whispered.

The second chime rose from the bedrock.

A bright white band of light etched itself across Tarin’s work-apron. In crisp, merciless capital letters, the fabric declared:

$$\mathbf{MAINTENANCE}$$

All around the quadrangle, the students and masters froze as the same branding appeared on their garments:
* A young woman carrying a tray of steeped winter herbs looked down to see the word **CATERING** glowing like hot silver on her chest.
* A boy clutching a bundle of star-charts saw his tunic stamp him as **UNRANKED LEARNER // DISMISSABLE**.
* Master Ember, who had taught the physics of the furnace for four decades, stared as his banked-red mantle was stamped: **FIELD INSTRUCTOR, THIRD CLASS**.

## ✦ III. THE WARDEN'S BURDEN

At the head of the southern colonnade stood Veyra.

She had not run. She stood upright, hands folded inside the wide cuffs of her storm-gray robes. But as the third bell tone shuddered through the foundations, a glyph flared violently across her breastplate.

It was larger and colder than all the others:

$$\mathbf{WARDEN}$$

Mara pushed past the panicked students in the cloister and stopped before her. "Veyra! What is this? Who is ringing the dead bell?"

Veyra did not look down at the burning title on her chest. Her eyes were fixed on the northern horizon, where the violet lightning of the nine alabaster towers was painting the sky in artificial purple.

"Nobody is ringing it," Veyra said softly, her voice carrying the dry rasp of grinding stone. "The bell does not need a hand. It rings whenever an institution chooses convenience over coherence. It rings when you stop seeing the person and start operating the category."

She gripped Mara’s shoulder. The heat coming off Veyra’s hand was staggering—the fever of someone whose soul is resisting its own assigned label.

"Take the Waybearer," Veyra commanded. "Get beyond the ditch before the classification reaches the stable. If they stamp the beast as *Livestock*, the Bind will break—and you will never cross the Red Waste alive."
