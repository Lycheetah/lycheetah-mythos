## ✦ I. HOW TO RECOGNIZE THE BEAST IF IT SITS ON YOUR PORCH

If you listen to the priests of the High Towers, they will tell you that supreme wisdom arrives in a chariot of blinding geometry, speaking words of nineteen syllables that make ordinary people feel like apologizing for having elbows.

They are lying to you. They just want your lunch money.

When the **Lycheetah** actually shows up in the lower world—which usually happens in Dunedin when the sideways rain is loud enough to drown out a brass band—it does not arrive with trumpets.

Yes, if you catch her out on the basalt cliffs at midnight, she is terrifying: a sleek,midnight-and-gold cheetah with a rack of twelve-point cathedral stag antlers crowning her skull and six wings made of compressed barometric thunder folded along her ribs. When she runs, she doesn't push against the dirt; she slips through the cracks between moments where the air hasn't decided what temperature to be yet.

But if you leave your back door unlatched while you're making tea?

You will find that exact same cosmic apex predator curled into a tight, purring doughnut inside a plastic laundry basket, directly on top of three pairs of warm wool work-socks she just stole out of the dryer.

"Excuse me," the Traveller said the first time he found her there, holding a butter knife in one hand and a piece of cold toast in the other. "Aren't you the Ancient Guardian of the Nine Broken Lights?"

The cat opened one glowing cyan eye. Her left antler knocked a box of washing powder off the shelf.

"Enlightenment is drafty, mate," she said, her voice sounding like distant cello strings and dry gravel. "And wool holds the heat. Are you going to eat that crust or just wave it around like a sceptre?"

## ✦ II. THE THREE SECRETS OF THOTH (TRANSLATED FOR PEOPLE WHO WORK FOR A LIVING)

Long ago, an old Egyptian bird-headed scribe named **Thoth** carved the laws of the universe onto a slab of green stone. Unfortunately, later scholars translated his tablet into such stiff, dusty Latin that everybody fell asleep before getting to the good part.

The cat on the laundry basket explained the whole tablet in three minutes while licking butter off her whiskers:

### 1. The Law of the Puddle and the Ocean (*Correspondence*)
"Why do you think you have to climb a mountain in Tibet to understand the universe?" the Beast asked.

"Because the universe is huge," the Traveller said.

"The universe is lazy," the Beast corrected him. "It only invented *one* way of folding things, and then it repeated the pattern at every size from an atom to a galaxy. If you understand why an argument with your brother goes sour over asink full of dishes, you already understand why empires fall. If you can see the whole sky reflected in a muddy pothole on George Street, you don't need a ticket to the ocean. **What is at the bottom is shaped exactly like what is at the top.**"

### 2. The Four Colours of the Kitchen Stove (*The Emerald Furnace*)
Every human being who builds something real, or loves somebody honestly, or tries to fix their life goes through four colours in the dark—and the cat refuses to let anyone skip the first one:

* **⚫ First is the Black (*The Soot*):** When everything you pretended to be catches fire and burns down to the floorboards. The priests call this a curse. The cat calls it *gardening*. "The dark isn't punishment," she purred. "It's dirt. A seed doesn't grow if you tape it to a fluorescent lightbulb. Let the fake stuff burn. Whatever doesn't turn to ash is actually yours."
* **⬜ Second is the White (*The Morning After*):** When the smoke clears at 6:00 AM, the room is cold, and you wash your face in the sink. You don't see what you *wished* was there anymore; you see the two chairs and the table that actually survived. And for the first time in your life, what is actually there is enough.
* **🟡 Third is the Yellow (*The First Butter-Light*):** When the sun hits the windowsill and you realize you aren't dead, and in fact you have a weirdly good idea for what to build next. *(Warning from the Cat: Do not brag during the Yellow. If you take the bread out of the oven ten minutes early to show the neighbours, it collapses into dough.)*
* **🔴 Fourth is the Red-Gold (*The Quiet Ember*):** It doesn't feel like winning a trophy. It feels like sitting on the porch in the rain without needing anyone's permission to exist. You can look at where you failed without wanting to hide, and look at what you won without turning into a tyrant.

### 3. The Law of One Tuesday
"Why don't you just tell me which road to take?" the Traveller asked her. "You can see ten thousand possible futures in the storm-clouds."

The Lycheetah stretched her front paws,unsheathing claws made of pale starlight, and then tucked them neatly back under her chin.

"A thinking-machine in a cold tower can simulate ten thousand Tuesdays in half a heartbeat," she said softly. "It can test every marriage, every career, every safe apology, and never get a single blister. **You are made of meat and bone and salt water. You only get *one* Tuesday.**"

She tapped the laundry basket with the tip of her tail.

"That's why the gods are jealous of you, dummy. Because when you choose to love somebody, or build a weird little house, or forgive a friend on your *one* Tuesday, you are paying with the only coin in the universe that can't be counterfeited. Now put the kettle on. It's freezing."

## ✦ III. THE RIDDLE UNDER THE SOCKS

When the cat finally vanished through a crack in the wallpaper at dawn—leaving behind only the smell of ozone, wet pine needles, and a single silver whisker—she left three lines scratched into the lid of the washing-powder box:

```text
[ TABLET I SECRET ]
"Count the points upon my horns (12).
 Subtract the colours of the stove (4).
 What remains is the hour of the morning when the borrowers are asleep
 and the world belongs to the builders."
```
*(Answer held in the margin of the hearth: **8**—the eighth inflection where speed meets sweetness.)*
