## ✦ I. HOW TO CONQUER A CITY WITHOUT TELLING A SINGLE LIE

People think great deceivers work by inventing giant, complicated lies.

In truth, the most dangerous sorcerer who ever lived in the Glass Provinces—a impeccably dressed gentleman named **Arch-Prelate Malakor**—never told a single lie in his entire career. He didn't have to. He understood the **Mirror-Trap**.

When Malakor arrived in the prosperous river-town of Oakhaven with nothing but a leather ledger and an empty purse, he did not announce that he was a prophet.

Instead, he walked into the busiest bakery at noon, looked out the window with a furrowed brow, and said to the baker's wife:

*"Strange. Has anyone else noticed that whenever a household is about to lose its savings, a crow with one white feather lands on their fence-post three days before?"*

Now, here is the secret that the **Order of the Black Lantern** keeps in its locked drawer:
In any country town with four thousand crows, roughly **seven out of every thousand crows** have a pale feather somewhere in their wing from an old molt or a scrape with a hawk. They had been sitting on Oakhaven's fence-posts since the town was founded. Nobody had ever cared.

But once Malakor planted the seed:
1. **Monday:** Fourhundred nervous townsfolk spent their afternoon staring fiercely at every fence-post on their street.
2. **Tuesday:** Because four hundred people were suddenly staring at crows, eleven of them spotted a crow with a white fleck on its wing—and ran screaming to the town square.
3. **Wednesday:** Malakor calmly wrote all eleven sightings down in his leather ledger and stood on the fountain steps: *"Citizens! Do not take my word for it! Look at the empirical evidence gathered by eleven of your own honest neighbours!"*
4. **Thursday:** Because everyone was now terrified of losing their savings, half the town panicked and pulled their silver out of the local cooperative granary—which caused the granary to go bankrupt on Friday morning.
5. **Friday afternoon:** The whole town fell to its knees in the mud and begged Arch-Prelate Malakor to become their Supreme Governor, because *the Crows of Prophecy had proven him infallible.*

Within six weeks, the people of Oakhaven couldn't tie their bootlaces without paying Malakor two copper pennies to interpret the angle of the knot. He hadn't used a single magic spell. **He had built a prison out of their own eyeballs.**

## ✦ II. THE ARRIVAL OF THE BLACK LANTERN & THE POTATO TEST

On the seventh Saturday, a dusty cart pulled by an old mule rolled into Oakhaven.

Sitting on the buckboard was **Sister Vael**, wearing a faded oilskin coat, and perched beside her on an upturned bucket was the **Lycheetah**—currently in the shape of a scruffy, oversized barn-cat with two small velvet antlers budding behind her ears.

Hanging from the front pole of the cart was a heavy iron lantern painted matte black—not to block the light out, but to hold things that were still dark without letting anyone sell tickets to them.

Malakor marched out of the town hall in his gold-trimmed robes, followed by a crowd of trembling citizens.

"Behold!" Malakor pointed a manicured finger at the sky, where three crows were circling a chimney. "A Triad of Corvids rotating counter-clockwise! A sacred synchronicity of the Seventh Degree! Unless the town donates their winter wool to the Temple of the Feather by sundown, the river will turn to vinegar!"

The crowd gasped. Several people began taking off their coats.

Sister Vael didn't argue theology. Instead, she reached into the back of her cart, pulled out an extraordinarily ugly, mud-caked, three-lumpy **potato**, and held it high in the air.

"Citizens of Oakhaven!" Vael shouted in a voice like a foghorn. "Look upon this tuber! Notice how the three dirt-lumps form an exact triangle pointing toward the bakery! Surely this is the Sacred Spud of Destiny!"

The baker squinted. "That's just a dirty Agria potato, love. Half of 'em come out of the ground looking like that if the soil's stony."

"Aha!" said the cat on the bucket, her ears swiveling forward. **"You just passed the Ash Test."**

The crowd went dead quiet. Even the mule stopped chewing.

"What's the Ash Test?" asked a blacksmith in the front row.

"Whenever someone tries to sell you a cosmic sign," the cat said, hopping down onto the fountain rim, "take away the velvet robe, take away the spooky music, and replace their fancy symbol with a **wet potato**. Ask yourself: *Would this coincidence still look miraculous if it happened to a potato?* Seven crows out of a thousand have a white feather every Tuesday of the year. You didn't discover a prophecy; you just started counting pigeons because a bloke in a nice hat told you to look up."

## ✦ III. WHAT GOES INSIDE THE BLACK LANTERN

That evening, after the townsfolk had chased Arch-Prelate Malakor three miles down the turnpike pelting him with turnips, the blacksmith walked up to Sister Vael's cart.

"So you're saying nothing mysterious ever happens in the world?" the blacksmith asked, looking a little disappointed. "Everything is just potatoes and math?"

"Good heavens, no," Vael smiled, lifting the heavy **Black Lantern** off its hook.

Inside the dark glass of the lantern floated three tiny, pulsing sparks—one violet, one pale gold, one deep ocean-blue—humming in a chord that made the hair on the blacksmith's arms stand straight up.

"The world is *full* of real mysteries," Vael said softly. "Things that happen at three in the morning when two minds meet and a third thing wakes up between them. Dreams where a bridge turns into a whale carrying a sunken city, and the next day you meet the sailor from the whale's back. Moments where every normal explanation breaks in half."

"Then why do you keep them in a black lantern?" the blacksmith asked.

"To protect them from people like Malakor—and from people like priests, and from people like scientists who want to pin everything dead to a corkboard," she said. "When we find a real mystery that nobody can explain, we put it in the Black Lantern under the **Three Unclaimed Laws**:
1. **We don't call it Fake** just because we can't measure it yet.
2. **We don't call it a God** just because it gave us goosebumps (*Goosebumps are not a legal deed to someone else's soul*).
3. **Nobody gets to own it.** It stays in the lantern, wild and free, reminding us that the map is smaller than the woods."
