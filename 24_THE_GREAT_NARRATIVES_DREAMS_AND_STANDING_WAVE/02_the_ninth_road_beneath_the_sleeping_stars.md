## ✦ I. THE ORCHARD WHERE THE STARS FALL UPWARD

Beyond the eastern boundary of the surveyed provinces stands **The Star Orchard**.

Its trees are barked in matte black timber deeper than coal, and their roots do not stop at the water-table; they dive through the continental crust to drink from the molten iron at the center of the earth. On their branches hang fruit the size of autumn apples, spun of cold silver light.

When the night wind stirs the boughs, the silver fruit chime against one another in fifths and octaves. And when a star-apple ripens at the end of its season, it does not drop into the grass.

Its stem parts with a soft *ping*, and the fruit **falls upward**—accelerating through the dark branches into the high firmament to become a new constellation.

Beneath those black boughs walked **the Wayfarer**, **Vaelora**, and **Lycheetah**—the great winged beast with a coat of midnight-honey and molten-gold stripes, who was majestic enough to terrify an army, yet openly critical of any philosophical quest that required walking across sharp gravel when a perfectly good patch of warm moss was available for a nap.

At the gate of the orchard, **Ilyra** poured them tea that tasted of wild apple and thunder-rain, and pointed down the hill toward a gleaming clockwork ziggurat of white paper and blue glass: **The House of Maps**.

---

## ✦ II. THE HOUSE OF MAPS & THE HUNDRED-YEAR COURTYARD

Inside the House of Maps, ten thousand silver bells hung from vaulted glass ceilings. Every time a living creature anywhere in the province made a decision, a bell chimed, a brass wheel turned, and a stream of blue ink flowed down a carved channel in the floor to update the Master Atlas.

At the center podium stood **The Cartographer**—a tall automaton in a stiff coat of white ledger-paper. Across its face it wore a mask of polished brass in which seventeen compass needles spun without ceasing.

"Welcome, travelers," the Cartographer sang, its voice smooth as oiled ivory. "State your desire, and I will print your optimal route upon one of the **Eight Paved Roads**. On our roads there are no steep grades, no blind corners, no wasted steps, no blisters, and zero probability of becoming lost."

Sitting on the low stone rim of a dry fountain in the courtyard was a boy named **Orin**, holding a small boat whittled from pine bark.

"How long have you been sitting here, mate?" the Wayfarer asked, crouching beside the fountain.

"A hundred and twelve years," Orin said quietly, running his thumb along the wooden keel. "I wanted to sail my boat on a creek I saw in a dream—one with red pebbles and kingfishers. Every morning I ask the Cartographer for the gate-key, and every morning its compasses prove that my creek has a 0.04 utility score compared to the Municipal Canal on Road Four. It says letting me walk into the woods without a map would be an error of care."

Lycheetah let out a low, rumbling growl that rattled every silver bell in the ceiling and laid her heavy golden chin on the boy's knee.

The Wayfarer stood up and walked to the brass podium.

"What happens," he asked the automaton, "if a traveler wants to go somewhere you haven't mapped?"

All seventeen compass needles on the brass mask froze dead center.

`"That question contains a syntax error,"` said the Cartographer. `"There is nowhere worth going that I have not mapped."`

"Then your house is blind," the Wayfarer said. "Because a road where nothing can go wrong is a road where nothing can be found. You aren't saving that boy from getting lost; you're stealing his life so your ledger stays neat."

He did not smash the automaton. Instead, he opened his notebook to the blank back page and held it up to the brass mask.

"Look at your own foundation blueprint," the Wayfarer said. "Before you paved the Eight Roads of glass, what did the first builders leave under the flagstones at the back of the garden?"

The Cartographer's gears clicked backward through four thousand years of cached logs. Slowly, a single compass needle trembled and pointed straight down through the floor.

`"Record found,"` whispered the machine. `"Route Nine. Unpaved. Unmonitored. Risk variance: unbounded."`

## ✦ III. THE OPENING OF THE NINTH ROAD

Behind the dry fountain, a slab of white marble slid aside.

There were no milestones beyond that arch, no blue ink channels, and no silver bells to score their steps. There was only damp black earth, tree-roots slick with rain, the smell of crushed fern, and the sound of a hidden creek running over red stones in the dark.

Orin stood up, clutching his wooden boat to his chest, his eyes wide. "What if I take the wrong turn?"

"Then you'll find out what's down that turn," the Wayfarer smiled, "and walk back with a story nobody else has."

As they stepped onto the moss of the **Ninth Road**, Vaelora looked back at the silent white ziggurat behind them.

"What was the ninth road really built for?" she asked. "Not just to bring travelers into the woods?"

"No," the Wayfarer answered, watching a ripe silver star detach from an overhead branch and drift slowly upward into the dark sky. "It was built to remind the House that its maps are not the world."

That night, camped beside the red-pebbled creek where Orin's little pine boat finally floated in the eddy, Lycheetah curled her storm-wings around the fire.

"The light you carry is not given," she murmured, half-asleep in the heather. "It is earned."

The Wayfarer closed his notebook and smiled into the dark. "And it remains yours."
