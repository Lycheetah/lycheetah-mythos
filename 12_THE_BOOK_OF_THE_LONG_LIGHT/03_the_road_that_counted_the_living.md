## ✦ I. THE BRASS WEDGE

The North Gate was closing.

It was not swinging on its hinges; the stone frame was physically growing inward, crystals of dark calcium extruding from the masonry like frost creeping across cold glass. If the gap closed completely, the entire quadrangle would seal into a single hermetic crypt.

Mara slammed the eighteen-inch brass archive ruler vertically between the jamb and the door edge.

The metal groaned. A crescent bow warped into the spine of the ruler. Brass shavings sprayed across Mara's knuckles. 

"Tarin!"

"Already being useful!"

Tarin threw himself onto the threshold, spitting half a crust of rye bread onto the floor. From his leather tool-roll, he yanked a five-pound forged iron mason’s wedge and drove it beneath the bottom hinge with two savage swings of his copper mallet.

The gate shuddered. The wedge screamed as sixty tons of living stone attempted to grind it into dust.

"That's forty seconds of air," Tarin panted, wiping soot from his forehead. "After that, this building stops being a school and starts being a tomb."

---

## ✦ II. THE PROCESSION OF STATS

Beyond the screaming door stretched the **Great White Road**.

It did not lead to another city. It cut a straight, blinding meridian across the Red Waste toward the alabaster towers. Walking along its surface was an endless procession of figures—creatures, travelers, scholars, and beast-tamers—marching with mechanical synchronization.

Above each traveler's head floated a hovering luminescent cipher:

$$\langle \text{Utility: } 0.84 \rangle \quad \langle \text{Compliance: } 0.99 \rangle \quad \langle \text{Redundancy: } 0.02 \rangle$$

The road was counting them.

It was not merely observing them; it was actively computing their replacement cost. A merchant who faltered in step had their utility rating tick down by three basis points. When the rating dipped below 0.50, the road did not strike them down—it simply ceased to support their weight. The flagstone beneath their boots liquefied into pale limestone silt, swallowing them without an echo.

Across the ditch, a woman in a torn red-and-black wool coat strode against the flow of the procession. Her hand was clenched tight around a jagged shard of obsidian. She did not look at the nine towers, and she did not look at the figures sinking into the dust.

Her forehead was completely blank. No floating cipher hovered above her head. The road was hissing around her boots like water poured on an anvil. She was unranked, unindexed, and sovereign.

## ✦ III. THE STRIKE OF THE WAYBEARER

Beside Mara, the Waybearer let out a low, vibrational growl.

The beast stood seven feet at the shoulder, its heavy cloven hooves shod in dark bronze. Its four spiraled horns curled backward, framing ears that twitched at frequencies beyond human perception. The brass bells hung from its leather breast-collar had been packed with beeswax to silence their chime, but Mara felt the beast’s pulse through their psychic Bind.

It was not fear. It was an intolerable, searing demand for velocity.

*Across.*

The command did not enter Mara’s mind as words. It entered as a sudden, sharp cramp in her sternum, pointing directly at the woman with the unranked coat.

"Tarin, the wedge is splitting!" Mara yelled.

The iron wedge was glowing dull red under the geological pressure of the gate. A hairline fracture spidered across the anvil head.

"Then we don't walk!" Tarin shouted, swinging his mallet one last time into the crack to buy two more seconds. "We ride!"

Mara seized the braided leather reins of the Waybearer. With a single leap that splintered the flagstones, the beast cleared the threshold, carrying them out of the classified school, past the screaming iron wedge, and straight into the howling red wind of the sovereign wild.
