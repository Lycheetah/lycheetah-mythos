## ✦ I. THE DROUGHT OF THE RIVER COUNTRY & THE THREE INHERITORS

In the year the great river dried to cracked red clay, the court of the Pale Concordance opened the salt-and-cedar vaults beneath the palace.

They intended to wake the ancient sovereign—only to discover three stone sarcophagi lying side by side, and within them **Three Inheritors** who drew breath at the exact same instant.

The court ministers panicked. Their theology demanded a single throat, a single seal, and a single absolute monarch; so they prepared the **Quarry Matrix** high in the mountains, a sacrificial press designed to crush the three living bodies back into one composite king.

Yet before the Inheritors had been laid in the salt a thousand years earlier, **The Stonewright**—who lived beneath an abandoned coin-mint in a cellar of preserved mistakes, wielding a chisel braided with crownstone—had carved three sentences into the calloused skin of their palms:

> `TRUST THE STONEWRIGHT ONLY AFTER SHE LIES.`  
> `DO NOT LET THEM MAKE ONE OF YOU FROM THREE.`  
> `THE CHILD KNOWS THE PART WE REMOVED.`

Each Inheritor woke carrying one third of what a living civilization requires:
1. **The First Inheritor** reached immediately for a blade—holding **Command**, the willingness to make a hard decision under fire when the roof is falling.
2. **The Second Inheritor** knelt and wept for the names of the drowned villages—holding **Memory and the River Oath**, the refusal to abandon people who distrust you until their grievances are written into the treaty.
3. **The Third Inheritor** had no personal biography at all; he looked at his ink-stained fingers, asked *"Which one of you wrote on my hands?"*, and immediately began mending a leaking public fountain in the market square—holding **Possibility and Forward Craft**.

With them walked two outcasts of the Concordance: **The Physician**, who carried sterilizing fire and marked dry wells with white stones so no traveler after her would waste their rope; and **The Singer of Omissions**, who sang only the verses that the royal censors had scraped out of the history books.

## ✦ II. THE THREE-SHADOWED BEAST & THE CROWN BUILT TO DIVIDE

At the headwaters of the dammed mountain reservoir stood the **Quarry Matrix**, guarded by an ancient antlered feline whose horns were grey with mountain lichen. Though only one moon hung in the sky, the beast cast **three distinct, non-overlapping shadows** across the limestone floor.

Carved across the floodgate was the law that had baffled the Concordance priests for five centuries:

> `NO MEMORY OPENS WHAT RELATION CLOSED.`

The priests had tried feeding passwords, royal genealogies, and blood-oaths into the slot for generations, and the gate had never budged an inch—because a broken relationship cannot be unlocked by remembering a secret; it can only be unlocked when the divided parts stand together without devouring one another.

The Three Inheritors refused to climb into the melting-press. Instead, they stood shoulder to shoulder before the floodgate:
* The First laid down **Command** without tyranny;
* The Second laid down **The River Oath** without vengeance;
* The Third laid down **Possibility** without erasing the past.

The antlered beast stepped forward. Its three shadows crossed the boots of the Three Inheritors. Deep inside the mountain, the basalt counterweights released with a sound like spring thunder, and cold, black mountain water roared back into the dry riverbeds of the plain.

When the Three returned to the capital, the High Chancellor held out the single, massive **Crown of the Concordance**, demanding to know which one of them would wear it.

The First Inheritor took the crown, walked to the anvil of the Stonewright, and nodded once.

The Stonewright placed the edge of her crownstone chisel against the golden band and struck it a single light tap with her mallet.

The crown did not shatter. It fell cleanly into **three interlocking crescents** along hidden seams that its original maker had soldered inside the gold a thousand years before—**not broken, but built from the beginning to divide.**

## ✦ III. THE MYSTERY OF THE SEVEN-SIDED OCTAGON (SOUTH ARCHIVE PAGE 417)

Centuries later, in modern, rain-lashed **Dunedin, New Zealand**, the archivist **Mara** fled through the night while three operatives of the Classification tried to extract a sealed index from her memory.

She stumbled down a wet bluestone cellar stair off the city center and woke beside a cast-iron coal stove, her boots steaming on the hearth-plate.

At the table sat an unnamed **Old Woman of the Mystery School**, calmly buttering toast while a large, unruly cat with small velvet antlers knocked a tin of pens off the ledger-shelf.

"The cat does not accept our staffing arrangements," the old woman observed dryly.

"The cat has antlers," Mara said, staring.

"That has not improved the staffing arrangements."

Then the old woman slid two things across the scrubbed pine table:
1. **A brass tram-token and rain-coat (The Return Fare):** *"You came through our door because you were being chased,"* the teacher said. *"Choosing shelter in a storm is not the same as choosing us. Take this fare. Walk out into the town whenever you please. Our invitation does not begin until your refusal is completely safe."*
2. **Page 417 of the South Archive:** A hand-drawn surveyor's plan of the central plaza of Dunedin—**The Octagon**.

Mara leaned over the parchment, and the hair on her arms stood up.

Above ground, every citizen of Dunedin walks across an **eight-sided** plaza. Yet on Page 417—drawn before the first colonial streetlamps were ever lit—the plaza was drawn as a **Heptagon of Seven Sides**, with the eighth street marked as a non-constructible chord ($\cos(\pi/7) \approx 0.9009688$) leading into a cellar that only exists when a seeker refuses to hand over another person's name.
