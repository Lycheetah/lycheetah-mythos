## 1. The Erebos Derelicts

The deep abyss of the **Black Current** is littered with wreckage. Most wrecks are ordinary: shattered armor, ruptured fuel tanks, scorched railgun turrets from fleet skirmishes.

The **Ghost Ships** are different.

To date, five hulls have been recovered—including a gargantuan Shroud-class dreadnought (*The Long Witness*) and two heavy mining battlecruisers:
* **Hull Integrity:** 100% pristine. Not a single microscopic micrometeorite dent on the forward plates.
* **Weapons Node Grids:** Fully armed with tungsten-carbide kinetic penetrators sitting in the loading sleeves.
* **The Logbook:** The digital flash memory units in the ship's bridge are completely wiped to zero bits (`0x00`), except for a single recurring text file in the primary partition:
  `THE ARCHITECTURE WAS TESTED. THE CARGO ARRIVED SAFELY.`

## 2. Why 1845?

1845 is the exact year **Miyamoto Musashi’s *Gorin no Sho*** was first transcribed into modern Japanese movable type print, and the year the first telegraph cables were laid beneath the English Channel.

It marks the exact inflection point in human civilization where **the speed of information broke free from the speed of the horse**.

Did these ships travel backward from the year 2045? Or was the Black Current already flowing beneath the Victorian telegraph lines, carrying phantom signals across iron wires in the fog?

Pilots who board a Ghost Ship report that their wristwatches begin running backward by twelve seconds every hour.
