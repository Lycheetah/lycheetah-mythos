## 1. The High-Compression Modes

When high-velocity founders communicate, long rambling paragraphs are forbidden. Ideas are compressed into lossless execution formats:

### Mode A: Triad-Zip (T-Zip) for Concepts
A concept is articulated in three tight lines:
* **Line 1 (Necessity / Protector):** What must be true? Why can this not be avoided?
* **Line 2 (Reciprocity / Healer):** What does the other party gain? How is this a non-zero-sum exchange?
* **Line 3 (Alignment / Beacon):** How does this directly serve the sovereign long-term purpose?

*If any line is hollow, the concept is rejected before time is wasted building it.*

### Mode B: Vector-Zip (V-Zip) for Decisions
* **Line 1:** Intended outcome (active verb, constraint named).
* **Line 2:** Non-negotiables (ethical floor, resource ceiling).
* **Score:** If estimated $SRS < 0.75$, swap verbs and constraints until integrity is restored.

### Mode C: Conflict-Zip (C-Zip) for Disagreements
When two engineers or founders reach an impasse:
* **Beat 1 (Steelman A):** State the strongest, most intelligent version of Position A.
* **Beat 2 (Steelman B):** State the strongest, most intelligent version of Position B.
* **Beat 3 (Vector Inversion):** Co-construct a third path that honors the invariants of both without compromise.

## 2. Vector Inversion: Ethics as a Steering Wheel

In legacy corporate governance, ethics is a brake pedal. It tells you: *"No, you cannot do that."*

In the Lycheetah framework, **Vector Inversion** turns ethics into a steering wheel:
* When an action violates an invariant (e.g. harvesting user data without consent), the system does not simply halt.
* It calculates the user's underlying intent, inverts the ethical vector, and generates 1 to 3 constructive alternatives that achieve the commercial goal **while raising the trust metric**.
* Refusal becomes creation.
