## ✦ I. THE INTELLECTUAL TRAP KILLED AT THE HEARTH

On July 18, 2026, Sol brought an ambitious proposal to the foundry table:
* It proposed turning the combat system into an epistemological simulator.
* Player attacks would be "Claims"; enemy armor would be "Dogma"; damage dealt would be calculated using the Truth Pressure formula ($\Pi$).

The Architect read the proposal and killed it on the spot:
1. **It Ruined the Fun:** A player playing an action RPG wants to dodge, time a counter-strike, land a heavy sword blow, and feel the thrill of victory. Turning combat into an academic debate destroys the kinetic joy of gaming.
2. **It Cheapened the Philosophy:** Making philosophical theorems function as cartoon weapons turns serious epistemology into a shallow gimmick.

---

## ✦ II. THE RULING: SEPARATE THE FIGHT FROM THE YIELD

The Architect's genius was in realizing that **the seam was at the loot table, not in the swing of the sword**:

* **The Fight Stays Pure:** Mechanical collision, damage numbers, responsive dodge-rolls, tactical telegraphs, and visceral adrenaline.
* **The Loot Becomes the Truth:** When a boss in the obsidian zone falls, it drops normal gear (armor, gold, potions), plus **The Yield**: a generated parchment fragment, an obscure cryptographic inscription, or a quest secret.

---

## ✦ III. "THAT COULD HOLD SOMETHING OR NOTHING"

The most profound design feature in The Architect's ruling is the explicit admission that a dropped secret **might hold something, or it might hold nothing**:

In traditional RPGs, every item has an immediate, unambiguous stat tooltip: `+15 Attack`.

In Lycheetah:
* You find an ancient scroll with an enigmatic coordinate and a fragment of non-commutative syntax.
* Is it a clue to the 13th Lead Hallway? Or is it the rambling note of a mad scholar who succumbed to the Black Current?
* **The only way to find out is to take the scroll, gear up, and walk the terrain yourself.**

This transforms **Truth Pressure into a playable mechanic**. The player doesn't read about epistemology; they *live* it. They must decide for themselves whether an idea is worth staking their time and character on.
